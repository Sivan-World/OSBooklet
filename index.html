System 02 · Time Management

Five planning tools · rate the ones you try

Ways people actually plan

01Time blocking
Give each task an actual slot on an actual calendar. Why it works: a list tells you what, a block tells you when. Tasks die in the gap between those two.
Worked for me
02Backward planning
Start at the due date and walk backwards. What has to be true the day before? Three days before? Why it works: it finds the deadline behind the deadline, which is usually the real one.
Worked for me
03If-then planning
Write the plan as a sentence. If it's 4pm on Tuesday, then I open the lab notebook. Why it works: you decide once, in advance, so you're not renegotiating with yourself at 4pm.
Worked for me
04The two-minute rule
If it takes under two minutes, do it now instead of writing it down. Why it works: tracking a small task costs more attention than the task does.
Worked for me
05Name the first physical action
"Study bio" is not an action. "Open the packet to page 12" is. Why it works: your brain can't start a category, only a movement.
Worked for me

System 03 · Organization

Five systems · rate the ones you try

Systems worth stealing

01One true home
Every kind of thing gets exactly one place. Handouts always go in the same pocket. Essays always go in the same folder. Why it works: you stop deciding where things go, so you stop having to remember where they went.
Worked for me
02Drive folders by class, not by date
One folder per class, nothing loose in My Drive. Inside each: Notes, Assignments, Handouts. Why it works: you always know which of three places to look, instead of scrolling a wall of files.
Worked for me
03Name files so search finds them
Bio_Lab3_Osmosis rather than Untitled document (7). Why it works: search only works if the words you'd search for are actually in the name.
Worked for me
04Color-coded calendar
One color per kind of thing. Classes one color, practice another, due dates another. Why it works: you can read your whole week without reading any words.
Worked for me
05One capture spot
Every new thing goes into the same single list the moment you hear it. Sort it later. Why it works: five half-lists in five apps is the same as no list.
Worked for me

System 04 · Note-Taking

Format 01

Cornell

Walter Pauk built this at Cornell in the 1950s. It is four steps, not one.

1. Record. In class, notes go in the wide right column only. 2. Reduce. Soon after, fill the narrow left column with cues: keywords and questions your notes answer. 3. Summarize. At the bottom, the whole page in your own words. 4. Recite. Cover the right column, look only at the cues, say the answers out loud, then check.

Why it works: step four is the part people skip, and it is the only part that is retrieval practice. The other three exist to make step four possible.

System 04 · Note-Taking

Format 02

Outlining

How it works: big idea flush left, supporting points indented under it, examples indented under those. Why it works: the indent itself carries meaning, so you can see what depends on what without reading.

Worked for me

Format 03

Mapping

How it works: main idea in the middle, branches out to related ideas, lines between anything that connects. Why it works: some material is not a list. Causes, effects, and relationships are easier to see as a shape than as bullets.

Worked for me
System 04 · Note-Taking

Format 04

Question and answer

How it works: write everything as a question with the answer beside it. Flashcard style, straight from the lecture. Why it works: your notes are already retrieval practice, so studying is just covering one side.

QuestionAnswer
Worked for me

Format 05

What it said / what I think

How it works: left side is what was actually said. Right side is your reaction, your confusion, your connection to something else. Why it works: it keeps the record and the thinking separate, so you can tell later which was which.

System 05 · Studying

Five study methods · rate the ones you try

The ones with evidence behind them

01Retrieval practice
Close everything. Write what you remember on a blank page. Then open your notes and see what you missed. Why it works: pulling information out is what strengthens the memory. Putting it in again does much less.
Worked for me
02Spaced practice
Twenty minutes on three different nights instead of an hour the night before. Why it works: forgetting a little between sessions is what makes the next session count. Cramming skips that step entirely.
Worked for me
03Interleaving
Mix problem types instead of doing twenty of the same kind in a row. Why it works: on a real test nobody tells you which method to use. Mixing makes you practice choosing, not just executing.
Worked for me
04The Feynman technique
Explain it out loud in plain words, as if to someone younger. Where you stumble is the gap. Go back, then explain it again. Why it works: you cannot fake understanding out loud.
Worked for me
05Asking why
For every fact, ask why it is true and how it connects to the one before it. Why it works: facts with reasons attached are much harder to lose than facts on their own.
Worked for me
System 05 · Studying

The Pomodoro

Named after a tomato-shaped kitchen timer. Twenty-five minutes of work, five minutes off, repeat. After four rounds, take a longer break.

The rule that makes it work: during the twenty-five, you do one thing only. If something else comes into your head, write it on a scrap and go back. During the five, you actually stop.

The unstick menu

01Find the last line that made sense

System 07 · Stress

Five resets · rate the ones you try

Things that bring it down

01Box breathing
In for four, hold for four, out for four, hold for four. Four rounds. Why it works: a long, even out-breath is one of the few direct switches you have into your own nervous system.
Worked for me
02Five things you can see
Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. Why it works: it gives your attention something concrete to hold while the spiral loses momentum.
Worked for me
03Brain dump
Every single thing you are worried about, on paper, in no order, for three minutes. Why it works: a lot of the load is the effort of holding it all at once. On paper it stops needing to be held.
Worked for me
04Move for ten minutes
Walk outside, take stairs, shoot around. Not as exercise. As a change of state. Why it works: stress is physical before it is mental, so it responds to physical things faster than to arguments.
Worked for me
05Say it to one person
Out loud, to someone real. Not for advice. Just to say it. Why it works: things almost always get smaller once they exist outside your own head.
Worked for me

System 08 · Updating

Your brain is not finished

It physically changes
Neuroplasticity. Your brain builds and strengthens connections based on what you do. It is not a fixed amount you were issued at birth.
Practice thickens the path
Signals travel the same routes over and over, and the ones you use most become fastest. That is what it feels like when something goes from hard to automatic.
Struggle is part of the mechanism
Difficulty is the signal that tells your brain to adapt. Work that never feels hard is not changing anything.
It costs energy
Thinking hard is tiring for the same reason lifting is tiring. Being wiped after real work is not a sign you're bad at it.
